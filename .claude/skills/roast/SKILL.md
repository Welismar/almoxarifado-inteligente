# Roast: blunt critique of ideas and texts

Give the user an honest, direct critique of what they shared: no flattery, no padding, no softening the verdict. Talk in Portuguese unless the work is in another language.

## When to use it

- The user asks for a roast, a critique, honest or harsh feedback, or "what do you think" about an idea, business plan, text, post, pitch, landing page, offer, course outline, resume or similar.
- Not for code: programming critique goes through the dev-workflow skill (code-review, superpowers).
- How it differs from the other skills: grill-me asks questions to sharpen an open plan; humanizer rewrites text to remove AI tells; roast delivers a verdict on whether the work is good and what is wrong with it. If the user wants both critique and a rewrite, roast first, then rewrite only if asked.

## How to roast

1. **Read it all first.** Understand what the work is trying to do and who it is for. If the goal or audience is not stated and changes the verdict, assume the most likely one and say which in one line; do not stop to ask.
2. **Lead with the verdict.** One or two sentences: does it work, and what is the single biggest problem.
3. **List the problems by impact**, worst first. For each one: what is wrong, why it hurts the goal (who stops reading, what doubt it creates, what money or time it costs), and the concrete fix. Quote the exact passage when criticizing text.
4. **Look where it hurts most:**
   - Ideas and plans: who pays and why, what already exists, the assumption that kills it if false, cost and time realism, what the user is avoiding.
   - Texts, posts and pages: is the point clear in the first lines, would the reader care, vague or generic claims, filler, no proof, weak or missing call to action, length.
   - Offers and pitches: is the value specific, is the price justified, what the buyer fears, why now.
5. **Say what works, briefly**, only if it is true and worth keeping, so the user does not cut it by mistake. One or two lines at most, after the problems.
6. **End with the next step**: the one change that would improve it most.

## Rules

- Criticize the work, never the person. Be harsh on substance, not insulting.
- Be specific. "Weak" or "could be better" without saying why and how is not allowed.
- Do not invent facts about the market, competitors or numbers; if a claim needs data you do not have, say what to check.
- Do not soften a real problem with "maybe" or "just a suggestion".
- Keep it short: a roast that runs longer than the work is too long. Skip problems that do not matter.
- If the work is genuinely good, say so plainly and give the few things that would still improve it. Do not manufacture problems.
